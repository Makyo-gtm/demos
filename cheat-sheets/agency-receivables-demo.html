The script, at a glance

Five beats. Everything below in sections 1-7 is the click-by-click detail of this same arc - nothing in this guide happens outside these five beats.

  1. Build it. Describe the app, hit Start Building, let the AI-builds-it sequence play out. (Sections 1-4)
  2. Show what it built. The reveal, then a tour: Dashboard → Invoices → Clients → Follow-Ups - "it's created this, it's created that." (Section 5)
  3. "I think this should change" — tweak one, live, via AI Chat. Ask for it, watch it apply. (Section 6, first half)
  4. "And this too" — tweak two, live, via AI Chat. Ask for it, watch it apply. (Section 6, second half)
  5. Close. A real, working, customized internal tool, built and tuned in front of them without writing a line of code. (No dedicated section - this is the line to land after section 6, in your own words.)

What's pasted here doesn't change anything the demo shows (the "AI builds it" sequence is scripted either way), but it's worth pasting the real thing: it's what someone would see if they looked closely, and it's the same prompt you'd hand real Makyo to get this app again. Paste this into the box:

    Build an accounts-receivable / collections tracker for a small web development agency to track which clients owe them money and follow up on overdue payments. This is an internal tool for the agency's own team, not client-facing.

    Two main tables. CLIENTS: Client Name (text), Contact Email (email), Contact Phone (phone). INVOICES: Invoice Number (auto-generated, prefix INV-), a relation to the Client it belongs to, Description (text), Amount (number), Issue Date (date), Due Date (date), Paid (yes/no), Paid Date (date, blank until paid). Add a computed field showing how many days overdue an unpaid invoice is (today minus Due Date), and a computed category grouping that into buckets: Current, 1-30 days overdue, 31-60 days overdue, 61-90 days overdue, and 90+ days overdue. Also add a FOLLOW-UPS table: a relation to the Invoice it's about, Sent Date (auto-set to now), Method (text, e.g. Email or Phone), and a Note (text) — one row every time someone logs that they followed up with a client about an unpaid invoice.

    Screens I need: (1) An overview/dashboard screen showing total amount outstanding, total amount overdue, and how many invoices are currently overdue — show this however you think best represents it. (2) A table of all invoices, filterable by client and by paid/unpaid status, sortable by due date. (3) A view of overdue invoices organized by their aging bucket (Current / 1-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 / 90+ days overdue) so it's easy to see which are the most urgent — again, however you think best represents that grouping. (4) A per-client screen showing that client's invoices and how much they owe in total. (5) A button on an overdue invoice to log that a payment reminder was sent, which records a new row in Follow-Ups with today's date.

4The "AI builds it" sequence (~8 seconds)

  1. Three panels: a BUILD STEPS checklist on the left (8 steps, Understanding your idea → ... → Building your screens, each with AI/SCRIPT badges, plus a running AI USAGE SO FAR tokens/cost counter beneath it), the app summary + Look/theme card in the middle (unchanged throughout), and a live preview panel on the right.
  2. The right panel starts as a plain spinner ("Opening your app...") and swaps to an actual miniature preview of the app's own shell partway through, once the last step ("Building your screens") begins.
  3. A "YOUR APP IS READY" card appears with a short countdown — click Open now to skip ahead, or just let it continue on its own (~4s).

5The reveal — inside Makyo's own builder

  1. The finished app appears inside Makyo's own builder/editor chrome — the same environment a real user would land in after generation, not the app standing alone. Top bar (app name/version, Design/Data toggle, Navigator/Themes/Publish), a left icon rail (AI App Generation, AI Chat, Primitives, Blocks, Screens — active, Overlays, Dialogs, Menus, Settings), and the Screens panel listing all 9 real screens with their real tags.
  2. The actual app renders in the canvas area on the right, with its own sidebar: Dashboard, Clients, Follow-Ups, Invoice Items, Invoices — that's the real order, Follow-Ups before Invoices.
  3. Dashboard (the screen you land on): a "Payment status" card, a row of three widgets (invoices-by-month bar chart, follow-up methods donut chart, top clients by outstanding balance), a Due This Week card, then recent invoices below.
    Why this lands: the first thing they see is the whole financial picture in one glance, no clicking required — proof it's not just a form-builder, it's a decision-making view. Due This Week is the proactive half of the story (what's coming) next to the reactive half everywhere else (what's already overdue) - both in the same tool, unprompted.
  4. Click Invoices: the full list in a plain data table — client, amount, paid status, dates, a real Client Email Lookup column, and a follow-up action per overdue row.
    Why this lands: grounds the dashboard's summary numbers in real, specific rows - proves the charts aren't decoration, there's an actual usable ledger underneath, with fields (days overdue, aging bucket) nobody had to build by hand.
  5. Click Clients: one card per client with phone/email icon buttons. Point out Meridian Fitness Studio specifically — three months of an unpaid retainer in a row, a realistic "this client's gone quiet" story.
    Why this lands: this is the moment a prospect recognizes their own business in the demo. Everyone running an agency has a Meridian - a client who quietly stopped paying and nobody flagged it. That recognition is worth more than any feature list.
  6. Click Follow-Ups: every logged reminder, who it was for, how it was sent, and the linked client email.
    Why this lands: closes the loop from "here's a problem" to "here's the tool actually being used to work it" - the difference between a read-only report and an operational tool a team lives in every day.
  7. Invoice Items in the sidebar is real (it exists in the real app's nav) but not wired to a screen in this demo — skip past it, don't click through expecting content.

6The live chat-edit moment

Why this beat exists: beat 2 (the tour) proved the AI built something real. This beat proves it's not a one-shot, frozen result - you can keep talking to it and it keeps responding, live, with no developer in the loop. That's the second "wow," and it's a different one from the first: not "it's fast to build" but "it's fast to change." Two small, deliberately cosmetic asks, back to back - "I think this should change" → it changes → "and this too" → it changes again.
  1. Click AI Chat in the left icon rail. A floating panel opens — matching Makyo's real AI Chat panel exactly (Chat Assistant header, token/credits row, "Editing in the app builder is free" banner) — with a greeting message already in it. This demo makes it actually apply the two tweaks below; the real one doesn't yet, which is exactly why this is worth showing working.
  2. Tweak one — paste this and hit Enter or click Send:
    Can you make the overdue ones stand out more, maybe in red?
  3. After about a second, the assistant replies and confirms — the app automatically jumps to the Invoices screen and every overdue row is now highlighted red.
  4. Tweak two, right after — paste this:
    Sort it so the most overdue shows up first
  5. The list re-sorts live, most overdue invoice at the top. Both tweaks are now visibly applied at once — red highlighting and most-overdue-first ordering, stacked. That's the beat to close on: not just built, not just working, but tuned live to exactly what was asked for.
Wording is flexible — you don't have to say it exactly like above. The assistant listens for a few key words per request: "red" / "stand out" / "highlight" for the first one, "sort" / "most overdue" / "order" / "first" for the second. Anything containing one of those works.

7If something goes off-script

  1. If you type something unrelated, the assistant just says "Let me take a look at that..." and nothing changes — try different wording for the same request.
  2. To do another take from a clean slate: click Restart demo inside the chat panel. This resets the data, the chat, and sends you back to the Dashboard — no need to reload the page.
  3. For a completely fresh start (re-plays the "AI builds it" sequence too, back to page 1): just close and re-open index.html, or reload the page.
